# Chinese Sentence Diagrams · Book 1 (HSK 1–2)
### 汉语句子图解 · 第一册

**See how Chinese sentences are built.** A free, offline web app that teaches the grammar of new HSK Levels 1 and 2 to English speakers. It draws every sentence pattern as a diagram, next to the matching English diagram, so learners can see exactly where the two languages part ways.

## Why diagrams?

English speakers rarely struggle with Chinese words. They struggle with **where the words go**. 我昨天在图书馆看书 puts "yesterday" and "in the library" *before* the verb, where English would never put them. This app makes that visible:

- **Chinese diagram.** Each sentence is drawn on a baseline (subject | predicate | object). Describing words hang below the word they describe, 了 / 过 / 着 hang in circles, and sentence particles float at the end.
- **English diagram.** The English translation is drawn in classic Reed–Kellogg form. The differences between the two diagrams are the lesson.
- **Word-order rail.** Fifteen fixed "stations" (Topic → Subject → Time → Adverb → Modal → Place → … → Object → Particle) show where every word must sit in a Chinese sentence.

## What's inside

- **115 sentence patterns** in **19 units**, covering the grammar items of new HSK (HSK 3.0) Levels 1 and 2.
- **For each pattern:** the sentence with pinyin and translation, both diagrams, the rail, a word-by-word gloss, the typical English-speaker mistake, notes on what's different from English, and a teaching tip.
- **Build it mode.** Learners place each word on the diagram themselves, with hints from the rail.
- **Unit practice quizzes**: put words in order, choose the right meaning, spot the mistake, and say which station a word belongs in.
- **Chapter 1: The word-order rail.** The fifteen stations explained, with exceptions to watch for.
- **Chapter 2: Reading the diagrams.** A short primer on English (Reed–Kellogg) diagrams for readers who never learned them, followed by the rules for the Chinese diagrams.
- **HSK checklists and word banks.** Every HSK 1 and HSK 2 grammar item, linked to the patterns that teach it.
- **Pinyin** marks the tone changes of 不 and 一 as spoken (bú qù, yí ge, yì běn).
- **Display options:** pinyin, tone colours, role colours, rail, gloss and English diagram can each be turned on or off. Any diagram can be saved as a PNG for slides and worksheets.

## Who it's for

- English-speaking learners of Mandarin at beginner level (HSK 1–2, roughly CEFR A1–A2).
- Teachers of Mandarin in schools: IGCSE, IB Mandarin ab initio, and NCEA and other school programmes.
- Anyone who wants to *see* why a Chinese sentence is ordered the way it is.

## Using it

- **Online:** open the link above in any modern browser, on a computer, tablet or phone.
- **Offline:** download `index.html` and open it. The whole app is a single file with no installation, no account and no internet connection needed.
- **In class:** project a pattern, hide the English diagram, and ask students to predict where each word goes before revealing the rail.

## About

Created by **Daniel Wang** · **Words THAT Think** (品词坊 · 以词启思). Vocabulary, grammar and literary analysis apps for IGCSE · A-Level · IB · AP.

The grammar scope follows the *Chinese Proficiency Grading Standards for International Chinese Language Education* (GF0025-2021). All example sentences, diagrams and explanations are original.
